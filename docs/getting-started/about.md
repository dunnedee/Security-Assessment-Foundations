# About & scope

## What this resource is

A free, independent study resource covering the foundational knowledge behind
penetration testing and security assessment, organised to follow the public
**CREST CPSA** syllabus. It's written as reference material: concept,
relevance, how it's assessed, and how it's remediated.

## How it's framed — and why

Every page is written from a **defensive, assessment-oriented** point of view.
You'll read about weaknesses in terms of *how they arise*, *how an assessor
identifies them*, and *how organisations fix them* — not as attack recipes.

This is a deliberate choice:

- It's how the knowledge is actually useful on the job. A good assessor's
  deliverable is a clear risk and a clear fix, not a trophy.
- It keeps the material suitable for a public, open audience.
- It reflects the professional and ethical standards expected of anyone working
  in this field.

Tools are named and techniques are explained at a conceptual level so you
understand what they do and why. The hands-on reps belong in your
[home lab](home-lab.md).

## Scope and limits

- This is **foundational** material. It is broad rather than deep, by design —
  the aim is a solid base to build on.
- It is **not exhaustive**. The syllabus is large and evolves; treat this as a
  living resource.
- It contains **no exam questions, recalled exam content, or reconstructed
  questions**. The self-study prompts are open questions written from public
  knowledge.

## Legal and ethical use

Everything here assumes a lawful, authorised context: systems you own, or an
engagement with explicit written permission. Applying these ideas to any other
system may be a criminal offence. See
[Legal & compliance](../assessment-management/legal-compliance.md).

## About the author

Maintained by **Mick Dunne**, a senior penetration tester and offensive security professional with over 15 years of experience in cybersecurity.

My background started in the British Army's Royal Signals, where I spent 12 years working in Electronic Warfare, Communications and Cyber Operations. Since leaving the military, I've built my career in offensive security, delivering penetration testing and security assessments across a broad range of technologies and environments. My experience spans traditional infrastructure, web applications, APIs, Active Directory, cloud, mobile applications and operational technology (OT), alongside more specialised and bespoke security assessments.

Along the way, I've gained several industry-recognised certifications, including Cyber Scheme Team Leader (CSTL), Offensive Security Certified Professional (OSCP), Offensive Security Web Assessor (OSWA), Certified Red Team Professional (CRTP) and CREST Registered Tester (CRT). I also previously held the Qualified Security Team Member (QSTM) certification before it was discontinued.

Today, I lead penetration testing teams across the UK and Ireland, but I still enjoy getting hands-on with the technical side of security.

I created this site to share some of the knowledge, tools and techniques I've picked up along the way. Whether you're just starting out, studying for certifications or looking to sharpen your skills, the aim is to provide practical, straightforward learning material based on real-world experience.

!!! note "A note on the trade"
    These notes reflect general, publicly available knowledge and the author's
    own understanding. They don't represent any employer or client, and contain
    nothing specific to any engagement.

## Contributing

Spotted an error, or want to suggest an addition or clarification?

- Open an issue or pull request on the project's GitHub repository (link in the
  top-right of every page).
- Keep contributions in the same **defensive, conceptual** register — concept,
  relevance, detection, remediation.
- Please don't contribute exam-specific content, recalled questions, or
  anything you're bound by confidentiality or an NDA not to share.
