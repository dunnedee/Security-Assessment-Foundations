# Who this is for

This resource is written for people at the **start** of a security assessment
career. You'll get the most from it if you recognise yourself in one of these:

## Service leavers moving into cyber

If you're thinking about leaving the forces and moving into cybersecurity, this site should help you get a better understanding of how penetration testing works in the civilian world. From how engagements are planned and delivered to the methodologies and technical skills involved, the aim is to give you a solid foundation to build on and help you work towards your first certification, such as the CREST CPSA or any buckers course you can get for free.

## Career changers moving into security

Perhaps you're in IT support, networking, system administration, or software
development and you want to move into offensive or assessment  focused security.
You already understand how systems are *meant* to work; this resource helps you
build the mental model of how they *fail*, and how that failure is found and
fixed.

If you're coming from outside IT entirely, you can still follow along, but plan
to spend extra time on [Core technical skills](../core-technical/index.md) and
on networking fundamentals generally.

## IT professionals adding a security string

You may not want to switch careers — you want security to become part of how
you do your current job. Understanding how an assessor views your estate makes
you better at building and defending it. Read the remediation sections closely;
they're written with you in mind.

## People preparing for an entry-level assessment role

If you're working towards your first junior penetration tester or security
analyst role, this gives you the vocabulary and the conceptual grounding that
interviewers and colleagues will expect. Pair it with hands-on practice in a
[home lab](home-lab.md) — reading alone is never enough in this field.

## What you're expected to know already

Nothing is truly assumed, but the following will make everything easier:

- **Basic networking:** IP addresses, ports, the idea of clients and servers.
- **Comfort with a command line:** you don't need to be an expert, but you
  shouldn't be afraid of a terminal.
- **General computing literacy:** files, processes, users and permissions,
  what an operating system does.

If any of those feel shaky, that's fine — the
[Core technical skills](../core-technical/index.md) section is a good place to
shore them up, and there are excellent free foundational courses linked from
[How to use this resource](how-to-use.md).

## What this resource is *not*

- It is **not** a shortcut to a certification. It's study material that happens
  to be organised the way the syllabus is.
- It is **not** a collection of exam questions or "what to expect on the day."
- It is **not** a copy-paste toolkit. Tools and techniques are explained
  conceptually; the practical reps come from your own lab.
